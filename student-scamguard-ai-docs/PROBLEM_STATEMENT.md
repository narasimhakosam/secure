# Student ScamGuard AI — Problem Statement Brief

## 1. Project Title
**Student ScamGuard AI**

## 2. One-Line Pitch
An explainable AI assistant that helps students identify potentially fraudulent job, internship, scholarship, training, and placement opportunities before they click, pay, or share sensitive information.

## 3. Problem
Students receive digital opportunities through WhatsApp, Telegram, email, social media, job portals, and forms. Fraudulent opportunities can imitate genuine organizations and use urgency, unrealistic promises, payment demands, fake recruitment language, credential theft, or malicious links to exploit students.

The core problem is not only detection. Students also need a simple explanation of **why** an offer looks risky and **what they should do next**.

## 4. Proposed Solution
Student ScamGuard AI accepts a suspicious message and/or URL, analyzes it using NLP, URL intelligence, machine-learning classification, and deterministic risk rules, and returns:

- Risk score from 0–100
- Classification: Safe, Suspicious, or High Risk
- Scam category, when identifiable
- Human-readable warning signs
- Recommended safety actions
- Evidence/features that influenced the score

## 5. Target Users
- College students
- Fresh graduates
- Job seekers
- Internship applicants
- Training/certification seekers
- Placement-cell communities

## 6. Core Use Cases
1. Analyze a suspicious internship/job message.
2. Check a URL before opening it.
3. Understand why a message is risky.
4. Receive a recommended next action.
5. Learn common student scam patterns.

## 7. Success Criteria
The MVP is successful when a user can submit a message or URL and receive a useful, explainable result in seconds without needing cybersecurity knowledge.

## 8. Scope Boundary
The system is a **risk-assessment and awareness tool**, not a legal authority and not a guarantee that content is genuine. “Safe” means no major risk indicators were detected, not that legitimacy is proven.
